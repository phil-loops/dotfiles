Part of [[content-safety-epic]]; continues in [[content-safety-screening-desk-log]]. Today one clerk glances at each message and says "send" or "stop."

**The instruments.** Before anyone forms an opinion, code checks every message the same way: does the link's label match where it goes, is the reply address free-mail, is there hidden text, has this template appeared on other accounts. Deterministic, not conclusive.

**The notes.** A clerk writes three lines, quoting the message: who it claims to be from, whether it asks for credentials or personal data, whether it asks for money or for money to go somewhere new. "Can't tell" is a valid entry. The clerk never writes a verdict and never checks identity; the desk's records say whether this account may make that claim.

**The rulebook.** Written rules read the instruments, the notes, and the account's record: send, hold for a person, or stop. A held message reaches nobody. Thresholds sit on a separate card. A new rule may only hold; it earns the right to stop by testing on separately reviewed cases, and loses it if reviewers keep waiving it.
